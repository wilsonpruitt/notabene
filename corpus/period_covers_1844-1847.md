# Period Cover Specifications, 1844–1847

## The Original Reitzel Productions of Nicolaus Notabene's Phantom Volumes

*Image-prompt reference document. For generating period-accurate facsimile images of the 1844–47 Copenhagen first editions, for use as frontispiece plates inside the Wroot Press 2026 critical edition, as slipcase visuals, or as marketing assets. These are NOT specifications for the 2026 covers (see `cover_design_spec.md` for those); these are descriptions of what the books would have looked like as they left Bianco Luno's press at Reitzel's order in the 1840s.*

---

## General notes for period-accurate imagery

**Format**: most volumes duodecimo (small), Vol IV octavo gift-annual scale, Vol VIII quarto in the format of 1840s philosophical periodicals.

**Typography of the period** (1844–47 Copenhagen):
- Title pages set in Didone (Bodoni or Walbaum range), large display caps for the title
- Author and publisher in roman caps below
- Italic for subtitles and dedications
- Bianco Luno's house style: generous leading, wide margins on title pages, mournful black rules where decorative
- Long-s (ſ) still used in some settings; double-aa (aa) for å (the å diacritic not standard until later in the century)
- Publisher's imprint at foot: "KJØBENHAVN. FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL. TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI."

**Binding conventions of the period**:
- Gift-annuals (Vol IV): cloth or paper boards with gold stamping, gilt edges, silk ribbon for hanging on the Christmas tree
- Speculative works (Vols I, III, VIII): paper-over-board or paper covers, modest production
- Sermon collections (Vol VI): dark cloth, blind-stamped religious device, sober
- Society publications (Vol V): paper covers in the society's adopted color
- Lyric/anonymous (Vol II, VII): plain paper covers, no ornament, hand-numbered

**Wear and aging for facsimile images**: light foxing on the cream paper; a thin patina on the gold stamping; a slight rubbing at the corners and along the spine; the silk ribbons faded from their original midnight-blue or pale-blue. The publisher's price-stamp in pencil on the inside front cover, if shown.

## Vol I — *Det logiske System*

**Title page (exact)**:

> DET LOGISKE SYSTEM
> *En Nytaarsgave*
> tilegnet
> **Det høistærede Læsende Publikum**
> første Bind, indeholdende §§ 1–4 samt Anhang
> *udgivet af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> Til Nytaar 1845

**Physical**: duodecimo, that the volume may rest comfortably in the palm of a young person to whom it is presented across the breakfast table on the morning of the New Year. **Bound in white kid**, with the title stamped in **deep gold**. On the back board, a single ornament: **a serpent biting its own tail** (ouroboros), around which is set, in Roman capitals, the motto **SYSTEMA EST**. Edges gilt to a depth which the binder warns will not survive a second reading. **Silk ribbon of pale blue**, sewn into the binding, that the volume may hang from the upper branches of the Christmas tree without the application of an external string. Interior set in a roman of unusual generosity; margins wide enough to permit the cultivated reader to enter, in the white space, further paragraphs of the System as they appear in subsequent annual volumes. A small **printed gold star** in the lower right-hand corner of page 47.

**Price**: 5 Rdl. in forgyldt Futteral with silk ribbon; 1 Rdl. 4 Mark stitched.

**Image-generation note**: a precious, slightly fragile-looking gift-book — the kind of thing a Bredgade household would buy a young niece for her name-day. The white kid catches the light. The gold has the depth of period gilt rather than the flatness of modern foil. The serpent is drawn in the engraver's manner of the 1840s — slightly stylized, more ornamental than naturalistic.

## Vol II — *Lyriske Productioner*

**Title page (exact)**:

> LYRISKE PRODUCTIONER
> *for hiin Enkelte*
> efterladte af en Forfatter, der ikke mere ønsker at være Forfatter
> *udgivne med Forord af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> Foraaret 1846
> *Oplaget er sat til femtenhundrede Exemplarer, hvoraf intet er bestemt for Anmeldelse.*

**Physical**: a small octavo, of the kind which fits without protrusion into the inner pocket of a coat. **Paper covers of a soft grey-blue, the colour of a Sound morning before the wind has come up** — the dye matched to the wrappers of the *Selskab for kvindelig Studie og Lectüre*. Front cover bears no title, no ornament, no author's name; only, at the lower right, **a small printed numeral in Roman type, hand-inked from the publisher's chamber, indicating the copy's place in the edition** (1 through 1500). The reverse of the front cover bears, in a smaller roman: *til hiin Enkelte* — followed by a blank space the size of a name-card, into which the recipient is invited to enter, in his own hand, his own name. Pages of honest white paper, generously margined. Type set with unusual leading. Four blank leaves at the end. Sewn with a thread of the same soft grey-blue as the covers; no ribbon. The spine is unmarked. The volume, set down upon a table among other volumes, cannot be distinguished from a notebook.

**Price**: not fixed. The publisher accepts whatever the reader, having read, chooses to send.

**Image-generation note**: deliberate plainness; reads as a *notebook* more than a publication. The grey-blue is muted and matt. The hand-inked numeral in the lower right is the only mark of individuation. Image should communicate intimacy and modesty rather than book-as-object.

## Vol III — *Det logiske System, Anden Udgave*

**Title page (exact)**:

> DET LOGISKE SYSTEM
> *En Nytaarsgave*
> første Bind, indeholdende §§ 1–4 samt Anhang
> **Anden forøgede og forbedrede Udgave**
> med en Anmeldelse aftrykt fra *Fædrelandet* samt en ny Anmærkning om Bind II
> *udgivet af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> Til Nytaar 1846
> *Pris: 3 Mark, eller en ottende Deel af Bogladeprisen paa første Oplag.*

**Physical**: duodecimo, as Vol I. **The white kid of the first edition has been replaced by a stiff paper-board, printed in white with a creditable imitation of the original kid grain.** **The gilt is gone.** The serpent biting its own tail, which on the first edition was stamped in deep gold on the back board, is here stamped in **matte black**, with the motto SYSTEMA EST set beneath it in a roman of slightly smaller body. The silk ribbon has been retained, but **in cotton** rather than silk, in the pale-blue shade of the original. The interior is set from the original plates, unaltered; the cultivated reader who possesses the first edition will find the type, the page, and the paragraph the same. The page numbers have been preserved. The small printed gold star on page 47 of the first edition has, in this edition, been printed in **black** rather than gold.

**Price**: 3 Mark — one-eighth of the bookshop price of the first edition.

**Image-generation note**: visibly a *cheaper printing*. The paper-board imitates kid but doesn't fool the eye. The black ornament where there was gold is the joke. Wear should be slightly more advanced than on Vol I — this is the popular edition that circulated in the smaller parlours, was handled by children, used as a presentation gift in households of modest means.

## Vol IV — *Urania*

**Title page (exact)**:

> URANIA
> Aarbog for 1845
> *udgiven af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI

**Physical**: small octavo (gift-annual scale). **White boards, gold ornament**: a single stylized **comet trailing across the front, its tail forming the letters U-R-A-N-I-A**. Thick paper, every page framed by a **black mourning rule** (kept from the previous volume of the gift-annual). The gilt edges have been deepened a shade. **A silk ribbon of midnight blue is bound in**, that the volume may hang from the Christmas tree without indignity.

**Price**: 4 Rdl. bound; 3 Rdl. 2 Mk. heftet.

**Image-generation note**: this is the *only* phantom volume that genuinely sold — it should look like a prestige object. The comet is the strongest single visual element of the entire phantom corpus; its tail forms the title in a fluid, decorative line. Period gift-annuals of the 1840s in Copenhagen — Heiberg's own *Urania* of 1844, *Gæa*, *Salonen* — used elaborate gold work on cream or white boards. This sits among them. The midnight-blue ribbon is the period's mark of a volume meant to hang. Light foxing acceptable; the corners should show wear from a decade of being taken down from shelves and Christmas trees.

## Vol V — *Tale for Total-Afholdenheds-Selskabet*

**Title page (exact)**:

> TALE
> *holdt for*
> TOTAL-AFHOLDENHEDS-SELSKABET
> *paa Selskabets første Aarsdag, den 8de October 1844*
> *af et virksomt Medlem*
> *nu, paa Selskabets ærede Bestyrelses Foranstaltning, udgiven ved Trykken*
> med Bilag indeholdende Selskabets Statuter, en Fortegnelse over de virksomme Medlemmer, Vidnesbyrd af Medlemmer, samt Indbydelse til Subscription paa de fortsættende Underselskaber
> *forsynet med et kort Forord af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> *Solgt til Indtægt for Selskabet. Pris: 1 Rdl., hvoraf de 4 Mark tilfalde Selskabets Drifts-Fond.*

**Physical**: octavo. **Paper covers of an austere blue**, the colour adopted at the Society's founding meeting after some discussion as to whether a colour ought to be adopted at all. The front cover bears **the Society's emblem in black**: **a cup inverted, beneath it the motto INTET DRAABE, and around the whole a wreath of oak leaves**, the wreath being added against the wishes of the Treasurer, who held that the wreath savoured of pagan festivity. Pages of honest if slightly coarse paper. The text of the speech is set in clear roman; the bylaws in a smaller roman; the testimonials in italic. **Bound in blue thread** of the kind used by the better class of bookbinders in Copenhagen. The volume hangs not from a Christmas tree but from the door of the Society's meeting-hall, where a copy is suspended for the use of any passing citizen who wishes to consult it before being moved to subscribe.

**Price**: 1 Rdl. (4 Mark to the Society's *Drifts-Fond*).

**Image-generation note**: this should look like an actual 1840s temperance pamphlet — not a literary work, an institutional publication. The blue is the deliberate "austere" blue of a society's adopted color. The cup-and-wreath emblem is centered, black on blue. Less polished production than the gift-annual volumes; the paper is rougher; the binding is the cheap thread-stitched style of pamphlets meant for circulation rather than display.

## Vol VI — *Fire og Tyve Prædikener*

**Title page (exact)**:

> FIRE OG TYVE PRÆDIKENER
> *til Opbyggelse for de Dannede*
> *forfattede af*
> **MAGISTER THEOPHILUS SOPHIENSEN**
> *Sognepræst ved en af Hovedstadens Kirker*
> *udgivne med Fortale af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI

**Physical**: octavo. **Bound in dark plum cloth** with a single **Greek cross blind-stamped at the centre of the front board** — no gilding, the Magister having let it be known that he wished the volume to be sober. Pages of a heavy white paper, ruled with a thin line in the upper margin in the manner adopted by certain liturgical publications. The titles of the sermons are set in small caps; the biblical texts are set in italic and translated, where the Magister judged the cultured reader incapable of unaided Greek, into a Danish so Hegelian-sounding that the original is sometimes hard to recover. **Bookmark of black silk**, sufficient for one reading per Sunday over six months.

**Price**: 3 Rdl. bound; 2 Rdl. stitched. Five percent discount for subscribers to the entire series.

**Image-generation note**: this should look like a serious mid-century theological work — Mynster's *Prædikener*, Martensen's collected sermons, or the period's standard Lutheran sermon-volumes. The dark plum cloth is the conventional color of theological publication. The blind-stamped cross (no ink, no gilt — visible only by relief) is the dignity of "sober" theology. The black silk bookmark hangs out the bottom. This is the most institutionally-respectable-looking of the eight period covers.

## Vol VII — *Philosophiske Smaastykker*

**Title page (exact)**:

> PHILOSOPHISKE SMAASTYKKER
> *eller*
> *hvad et Forord kan blive til*
> *naar Bogen tages bort*
> *af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> 1847

**Physical**: a small octavo, of the size which the publisher uses for ordinary devotional literature. **Paper covers, the colour of unbleached linen, without ornament of any kind.** The title set on the front cover in a plain roman, the lines descending without indentation; beneath the title, the author's name, set in the same roman, in a body one degree smaller. **No motto. No engraving. No allegorical figure. No silk ribbon, no cotton ribbon, no ribbon.** The spine bears the title only, in the same roman, running upward. Pages of honest white paper; type set without leading beyond the customary; **narrower margins** than in the lyrical productions of the preceding year. The volume opens at the spine without resistance; bound in the manner of a workman's almanac, that it may lie flat upon a table without being held open.

**Price**: 1 Rigsdaler, the same in stitched covers and in boards.

**Image-generation note**: the *plainest* of the eight period covers. Unbleached linen paper — a beige-tan, mottled, with a visible coarse weave or fiber structure. No ornament whatsoever. The title set austerely. This should read as a workman's almanac or a minor devotional tract, not a literary publication. Among the period covers, it should be the most uncomfortable to look at — defiantly unornamented, signaling its argument by its appearance.

## Vol VIII — *Philosophiske Overveielser, Første Hefte*

**Title page (exact)**:

> PHILOSOPHISKE OVERVEIELSER
> Et Tidsskrift
> *Første Hefte*
> *udgivet af*
> **NICOLAUS NOTABENE**
> KJØBENHAVN
> FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL
> TRYKT I BIANCO LUNOS BOGTRYKKERI
> Januar 1845
> *Subscriptionspriis for fire Hefter: 4 Rdl. 3 Mark. Enkelt Hefte: 1 Rdl. 2 Mark.*

**Physical**: **quarto**, in the format adopted by Heiberg's *Perseus* and other Copenhagen philosophical periodicals, that the present journal might be shelved beside its predecessors without disturbing the symmetry of the cabinet. **Pale grey paper covers**, the title in a heavy roman, the editor's name beneath in italic, the issue number ("Første Hefte") in small caps. **No ornament.** The editor has been advised by his publisher that an emblem would assist sales, and has agreed in principle, but has not yet found an emblem he could accept; the matter is deferred to the second issue. Pages of a serviceable white paper, generously margined for the convenience of those readers who wish to enter their corrections in the margin and return the issue to the editor. The typeface is set somewhat larger than is customary, in deference to the editor's hope that several of his expected correspondents are advanced in years.

**Price**: 1 Rdl. 2 Mark per Hefte; 4 Rdl. 3 Mk. for the full year's subscription.

**Image-generation note**: the only quarto in the corpus — substantially larger and broader than the other seven. Pale grey paper covers, very plain, in the format of a contemporary philosophical journal like Heiberg's *Perseus* (1837–38) or *Maanedsskrift for Litteratur*. The large typography on the cover, the journal's table of contents partially visible through the wrapper — communicates "this is a periodical, not a book." Slightly more aged appearance than the books, since journals were handled more roughly. A wrap-around band or seal indicating the issue number could be shown.

## Reception-history layer (deferred)

A *third* tier of visual material exists conceptually but has not yet been specified: the 19th- and 20th-century editorial-history covers that Henriksen's apparatus references. These would be used inside the 2026 critical-edition apparatus as small reproduction plates, illustrating the volumes' transmission. They include:

- **1849** Berlin Vogel piracy of Vol VI — *Vierundzwanzig Predigten für die Gebildeten* (Biedermeier theological publication style)
- **1858** F. Storm, Århus, reprint of Vol V (provincial Danish pamphlet style)
- **1874** Schubothe popular edition of Vol IV (reduced production values; section V omitted)
- **1887** *Linköpings Nykterhetsförening* Swedish translation of Vol V — *Tal hållet vid Total-Afhållsamhets-Sällskapets årsmöte* (Swedish temperance pamphlet)
- **1888** Schubothe *Folkeudgave* of Vol I, ed. F. P. J. Dahl (without the *Anhang*) (late-Victorian Danish cheap-classic style)
- **1928** Haecker German translation of Vol VII (Innsbruck *Hochland* series, plain modernist)
- **1934** Bertelsen-Hertel first edition of Vol VIII *Andet Hefte* recovered from the Reitzel attic (Gads Forlag interwar scholarly style)
- **1948** Lowrie English of Vol VII — *Philosophical Pieces* (Princeton University Press 1940s academic)
- **1958** Ulla Bruun de Neergaard's bookplate from copy no. 0837 of Vol II (Royal Library accession plate)
