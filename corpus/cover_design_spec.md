# Cover and Production Spec

## Wroot Press 2026 Kierkegaard–Notabene Edition

*Working spec, drafted 2026-05-14 (evening); revised 2026-05-14 to separate the 2026 critical-edition direction from the 1844–47 period descriptions.*

**Scope of this document**: the 2026 Wroot Press critical-edition direction *only*. This is what the actual physical books in the boxed set will look like, in modern scholarly-edition language (clean serif typography, period ornaments deployed deliberately, Henriksen credited as editor, series mark uniform across the eight volumes).

**For image generation of the original 1844–47 Reitzel productions** — for use as frontispiece plates inside the 2026 volumes, slipcase visuals, marketing assets, etc. — **see `period_covers_1844-1847.md`**, the companion document. That file contains the period-accurate descriptions of each volume as it would have appeared in 1844, 1845, 1846, or 1847; it is image-prompt-ready and avoids the modern critical-edition framing entirely.

The two documents are intended to be used together: the period file describes what the books *were*; this file describes what the 2026 edition *makes* of them.

---

## I. Editorial framework

**Series title (Danish)**: *Forord & dets fantom-bind*
**Series title (English)**: *Prefaces and their Phantom Volumes*
**Subtitle**: *Critical edition of the works of Nicolaus Notabene, 1844–1847*

Each of the eight phantom volumes appears as a single physical book containing:

1. *Editor's introduction* (English), specific to that volume's recovery history. ~1,200–1,800 words. Sober Cappelørn-style register; the editor is invisible-professional; jokes live in what the apparatus *records*, not in editorial voice.
2. *Bilingual body*: Danish facing-page left (period-typography reset), English facing-page right.
3. *Textual apparatus* (back matter): variants, manuscript history, witnesses, per-volume recovery details.

The lost/found play is carried by the introductions and apparatus. Covers and body remain clean.

## II. The 2026 critical editor

**Name**: Mads Fedder Henriksen, ph.d.
**Affiliation**: *Adjunkt, Søren Kierkegaard Forskningscentret, Københavns Universitet*

A working academic at a real Copenhagen institution. The name carries no in-joke; the persona is meant to be invisible-professional. His brief biographical note appears in the colophon of each volume:

> Mads Fedder Henriksen (b. 1976) is Adjunkt at the Søren Kierkegaard Forskningscentret. His prior work includes contributions to *Søren Kierkegaards Skrifter* and a 2019 monograph on the Heiberg circle. The present edition of the Notabene corpus is his first sustained editorial undertaking outside the SKS.

He signs each editor's introduction and the textual apparatus. He does not appear in the cover ornament.

## III. Design system (uniform across the series)

**Format**: octavo, 4½ × 7 in. (114 × 178 mm). Volume VIII is the single exception: quarto, in the format of the 1840s philosophical periodicals (see VIII below).

**Cover material**: paper-over-board, matte finish, lightly textured ivory stock as the base; ground color applied as a tinted wash so the texture remains visible through the color. Exception: Vol VI in cloth, to match the slipcase.

**Page count**: 80–180 per volume after bilingual layout; VII the longest, II the shortest.

**Typography**:
- Cover and title-page display: Lyon Display, or Caslon (period-evoking but modern)
- Body Danish: a contemporary Didone (Bodoni or Walbaum range) reset of the 1844 period typography
- Body English: Lyon Text or Whitman
- Series mark and small text: a humanist sans (Brandon, Whitney, or similar)

**Cover architecture** (uniform):

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  WROOT PRESS                  I │   series mark + roman numeral
│  KIERKEGAARD–NOTABENE EDITION   │
│                                 │
│                                 │
│      [DANISH TITLE]             │   title, large
│      [ornament]                 │   original 1844 device,
│      [Danish subtitle]          │   in period color
│                                 │
│      [English title]            │   English block, slightly smaller
│      [English subtitle]         │
│                                 │
│      NICOLAUS NOTABENE          │   author small caps
│                                 │
│      ─────────────────          │
│      Edited and translated      │
│      by Mads Fedder Henriksen   │
│                                 │
│      W R O O T   P R E S S      │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

Spines: ground color of the volume; series title and roman numeral in gilt running upward; Wroot Press device at the base.

Bilingual title pages inside: Danish title page on verso (period typography reset, no apparatus), English title page on facing recto (modern critical-edition typography, with editor's name beneath).

## IV. Per-volume cover briefs

*Each brief below describes the 2026 critical-edition cover for that volume — the ground color, the ornament's modern treatment, and any recovery-history signal carried on the cover. The original 1844–47 Reitzel production each cover honours is documented in full in `period_covers_1844-1847.md`. References in the briefs below to "the 1845 first edition" or "the 1846 popular printing" should be read as pointing to that companion document, not as descriptions of the 2026 cover itself.*

### Vol I — *Det logiske System*

**Ground**: warm white paper-over-board (slightly off-white, not bright).
**Ornament**: the ouroboros stamp from the 1845 first edition, reproduced at quarter-scale in deep matte gold, set between the Danish and English subtitle blocks. Motto SYSTEMA EST in roman capitals immediately beneath, in the same gold.
**Title**: "DET LOGISKE SYSTEM" / "en Nytaarsgave" / "The Logical System" / "A New Year's Gift".
**Recovery signal**: none on cover; the editor's intro records the 1888 Schubothe abridgement and the manuscript Subscriber's Roll.
**Spine**: warm white ground, gilt title.

### Vol II — *Lyriske Productioner*

**Ground**: soft grey-blue paper, matte. The dye is matched, in the editor's note, to that of the *Selskab for kvindelig Studie og Lectüre*'s 1846 wrappers — a quiet historical citation.
**Ornament**: *none*. The 1846 originals were deliberately unornamented; the 2026 cover honours that.
**Title**: "LYRISKE PRODUCTIONER" / "for hiin Enkelte" / "Lyric Productions" / "for that Single Reader".
**Author**: not on the cover. The 1846 anonymous publication is preserved as a cover feature; the editor's introduction inside identifies Notabene.
**Special feature**: at the lower-right of the front cover, a small printed numeral in roman type, hand-inked in dark blue ink as in the 1846 originals (the copy number within the 1,500-copy print run). The 2026 reissue continues the numbering from 1,501.
**Recovery signal**: on the back cover, a small grey-blue debossment marks the position of Ulla Bruun de Neergaard's bookplate from the Kongelige Bibliotek copy.
**Spine**: grey-blue, gilt title.

### Vol III — *Det logiske System, Anden Udgave*

**Ground**: warm white, very slightly cooler than I — a half-tone off, signaling popular-reprint status. The buyer who shelves I and III side by side will perceive the difference.
**Ornament**: same ouroboros as I, stamped in *matte black* rather than gold (per the 1846 popular edition's reduced production values). Motto SYSTEMA EST in roman capitals beneath, also matte black.
**Title**: "DET LOGISKE SYSTEM · ANDEN UDGAVE" / "Second Edition" / "at one-eighth the price". The price-history is part of the title block — the only volume where this is so.
**Special feature**: a horizontal printed rule across the lower-front bears, at its centre, the year *1846* — flanked by short bibliographical ticks for *1888* and *2026*, indicating the volume's print history.
**Recovery signal**: the apparatus records that the *first* edition (I) is lost; the present volume is the only one of the eight whose physical first edition exists nowhere but is reconstructed from the second. This is signaled on the cover only by the matte-black ouroboros (i.e. the cheaper printing, which is the only one that survives).
**Spine**: warm white, matte-black title (not gilt) running upward. Of the eight spines, III's is the only un-gilt — a quiet visual reminder.

### Vol IV — *Urania*

**Ground**: white paper-over-board, slightly creamier than I.
**Ornament**: the stylized comet whose tail forms the letters U-R-A-N-I-A, reproduced at full scale in deep matte gold, occupying the upper half of the front. The title block sits below the comet, deliberately smaller than usual, to give the ornament its due.
**Title**: "URANIA" / "Aarbog for 1845" / "Urania" / "Almanac for 1845".
**Frames**: the mourning rule from the 1845 original is preserved — a thin black rule ¼ inch from the edge on all four sides of the front and back covers.
**Special feature**: a midnight-blue silk ribbon bound in. The *only* ribbon in the series, preserving Urania's gift-annual character.
**Spine**: white with deep gold title; a small gold star at the foot (replacing the page-47 device described in I's Anhang C — a cross-volume nod, easy to miss).
**Production note**: of the eight, this is the most ornamented cover, by intention. *Urania* was the only phantom volume that genuinely sold, and its cover preserves the 1845 production values to a degree the other volumes do not warrant.

### Vol V — *Tale for Total-Afholdenheds-Selskabet*

**Ground**: austere blue paper, matte, of the colour adopted at the Society's founding meeting. Slightly desaturated from the period swatch (which the editor's introduction reproduces in a frontispiece plate).
**Ornament**: the Society's emblem — an inverted cup with the motto INTET DRAABE beneath, set in a circular oak-leaf wreath — reproduced in matte black. The motto's lettering in a heavier roman than the rest of the cover.
**Title**: "TALE FOR TOTAL-AFHOLDENHEDS-SELSKABET" in tight roman capitals / "Address Delivered before the Total Abstinence Society".
**Special feature** (the Swedish trace): below the English subtitle, in slightly smaller italic, the Swedish title from the 1887 Linköping circulation: *Tal hållet vid Total-Afhållsamhets-Sällskapets årsmöte*. This signals the apparatus's three-text treatment, where the Swedish appears as a third column on pages where it diverges from the Danish.
**Binding**: bound with blue thread of the kind used by the better class of bookbinders, matching the 1845 description.
**Spine**: austere blue, gilt title.

### Vol VI — *Fire og Tyve Prædikener*

**Ground**: dark plum cloth-over-board. **The only cloth-bound volume in the series**, matching the slipcase. The cloth chosen to match Magister Sophiensen's specification that the volume be "sober."
**Ornament**: a single Greek cross blind-stamped at the centre of the front board — no gilding, no ink, the device visible only by relief.
**Title**: set in *small* type, positioned below the cross, the cross dominating. "FIRE OG TYVE PRÆDIKENER" / "til Opbyggelse for de Dannede" / "Twenty-Four Sermons" / "for the Edification of the Cultivated".
**Author**: "MAGISTER THEOPHILUS SOPHIENSEN" as on the 1845 original, with a smaller line below: "udgivet med Fortale af NICOLAUS NOTABENE / edited with preface by Nicolaus Notabene". The Notabene publisher-fiction is preserved on the cover.
**Special feature** (the German trace): along the lower edge of the front board, a small printed band in italic gives the 1849 Berlin piracy's German title: *Vierundzwanzig Predigten für die Gebildeten*. This signals the German variants reported in the apparatus.
**Binding**: black silk bookmark bound in (sufficient, per the 1845 description, for one reading per Sunday over six months).
**Spine**: dark plum, gilt title.

### Vol VII — *Philosophiske Smaastykker*

**Ground**: unbleached linen paper-over-board — the colour of a workman's almanac, exactly as Notabene specified.
**Ornament**: *none*. No engraving, no allegorical figure, no rule, no motto. The cover is austere by argument, not by oversight.
**Title**: "PHILOSOPHISKE SMAASTYKKER / eller / hvad et Forord kan blive til / naar Bogen tages bort" in plain roman. English block below: "Philosophical Small Pieces / or what a Preface becomes / when the Book is taken away".
**Author**: "NICOLAUS NOTABENE" set with no decoration, no italic, no rule. The only volume in the series where Notabene's signature stands without any qualifying scaffolding around it. The signature is the cover's chief gesture.
**Recovery signal**: at the foot of the front cover, in small roman: "Recovered 1883, edited 2026." The only volume that bears a recovery-date inscription on its cover. The 1883 = Brandes's notice in *Det moderne Gjennembruds Mænd*.
**Spine**: unbleached linen, gilt title. The gilt is the only ornament the volume permits itself.

### Vol VIII — *Philosophiske Overveielser*

**Ground**: pale grey paper, in **quarto** rather than octavo — VIII is the single format anomaly in the series, breaking the uniform octavo on purpose to honour the *Perseus*-format of 1840s Copenhagen philosophical periodicals.
**Ornament**: none.
**Title**: "PHILOSOPHISKE OVERVEIELSER" in heavy roman, with "Første Hefte" in small caps below; English subtitle "Philosophical Considerations / First Issue [with Second Issue recovered]".
**Special feature**: along the lower-front, a small printed notice — "Hefte II first published from manuscript, 1934. Hefter III–IV unrecovered." Signals the apparatus's transmission history.
**Volume mark**: "VIII" with an asterisk indicating the format anomaly; the asterisk's note appears in the slipcase insert.
**Spine**: pale grey, gilt title. The slightly greater height means VIII sits proudly distinct at the right end of the slipcase row.

## V. The slipcase

**Material**: deep plum cloth-over-board, matching Vol VI exactly so the slipcase reads as continuous with the cloth volume sitting inside it.
**Spine of slipcase**: gilt, running upward — "FORORD & DETS FANTOM-BIND · KIERKEGAARD–NOTABENE · WROOT PRESS · 2026".
**Foot of slipcase spine**: small Wroot Press device.
**Volumes visible in chord**: the eight spines arranged I–VIII present the following color sequence — warm white, grey-blue, warm-white-cool, white, austere blue, dark plum, linen, pale grey. The chord is part of the design; the buyer sees it the moment the slipcase is opened.
**Inserted card**: a single printed leaf, loose inside the slipcase, listing the volumes in *two* orders —
- Kierkegaard's order (I → VIII), as printed on the spines
- Notabene's biographical order (IV / I / VIII / V / VI / III / II / VII), per the chronology established in *notabene_bio_and_boxset.md*

The buyer chooses the reading sequence; the slipcase enforces neither.

## VI. The optional ninth volume

*Liv og Tid af Nicolaus Notabene* — biographical companion, ~10k words.

**Ground**: dove grey (an auxiliary color, distinct from any of the eight).
**Format**: octavo, matching I–VII.
**Series mark**: same, with roman numeral **IX**.
**Spine**: dove grey, gilt title.
**Position in slipcase**: optionally inserted between VII and VIII at the right end, or shelved separately. The slipcase is designed to hold eight; IX rides as a slim extra at the user's discretion.

## VII. Production notes

- *Bilingual page balance*: the editor should aim for English and Danish columns of equal length on facing pages. Where the Danish runs short (the eighteenth-century-style periods are often more compact than their English equivalents), the right-hand English margin absorbs the slack.
- *Period typography on Danish pages*: the Danish text should be reset, not photo-reproduced from the 1844–47 originals. The reset uses period typographic norms (ſ long-s for s; å set as aa; the Fraktur capitals where the originals used them) so the page reads as period without being a facsimile.
- *Page numbering*: continuous arabic across both languages; the apparatus references the English page only.
- *Frontispiece in each volume*: a single plate reproducing the 1844–47 original's title page (when extant) or a manuscript leaf (where the original is lost), printed on a slightly heavier stock than the body.
