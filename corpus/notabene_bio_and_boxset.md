# Notabene's Life & The Wroot Press Boxed Set

*Working sketch, drafted 2026-05-14 between Preface I and Preface III.*
*All dates and details below are proposals; revise freely.*

---

## I. The structural opportunity

The original *Forord* (Kierkegaard, 1844) gives us a framing fiction in which Notabene's wife has forbidden him to write books and permitted, by compromise, only prefaces. Each of the eight prefaces stands in front of a phantom volume that does not exist.

The Wroot Press edition makes the phantom volumes exist. To do this honestly it needs a fictional account of *how Notabene came to write them*, since the original framing makes their existence impossible. The most elegant available device:

> **The wife eventually leaves him. With the prohibition lifted, he writes the books the prefaces had been waiting for.**

This converts the prohibition into a *productivity engine* and gives the boxed set a dramatic arc rather than a flat anthology. It also produces a tonal hinge halfway through the corpus: volumes published *under the prohibition* (where Notabene must hide behind editor-fiction or publisher-fiction) versus volumes published *after the departure* (where he writes openly).

A further refinement: she leaves him not for adultery, not by death, but to **join a Selskab of her own** — a parallel to Preface V's Total Abstinence Society. Specifically, the *Selskab for kvindelig Studie og Lectüre* (Society for Female Study and Reading). The irony is sharp: she has gone away to *read other books in association with other women*, having for fifteen years forbidden her husband to write any. The form of voluntary association that Notabene satirised in V is the form that takes her from him.

## II. Proposed biographical chronology

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| ca. 1805 | NN born somewhere in Sjælland, modest gentry, cand. theol. by training but never ordained |
| 1828 | Engaged at Christianshavn |
| 1830 | Marries. She establishes the prohibition immediately — having heard him read aloud once during the engagement, she had found the experience intolerable and made his consent to silence a condition of consent to marriage. He had agreed without examining the terms |
| 1832–43 | Twelve years of attempted authorship. Manuscripts repeatedly confiscated; some used as embroidery underlay, some converted to hair-papers |
| 1843 spring | The "Indlednings-Paragraph" episode (recounted in Notabene's own *Forord*): she sets fire to his manuscript with a parlour candle. Notabene proposes the compromise — *prefaces only*, no books. She accepts, on the supposition that no one writes a preface without writing a book |
| 1844 June | *Forord*, eight prefaces, published under the Notabene pseudonym. He writes them in a borrowed room at the country estate of an aunt, his wife being told he has gone for the air |

### Phase I — under the prohibition (Notabene as editor/publisher only)

| Year | Volume | Notabene's role |
|---|---|---|
| 1844 Dec | **IV.** *Urania. Aarbog for 1845* | "Editor" (gift annual; multiple imagined contributors, the editor merely compiling) |
| 1845 Jan | **I.** *Det logiske System. En Nytaarsgave* | "Editor" (the System being, in principle, the work of speculative philosophy itself; NN merely sees it through the press) |
| 1845 spring | **VIII.** *Philosophiske Overveielser, Første Hefte* | Editor of a journal; the questions are his, the content is his correspondents' |
| 1845 spring | **V.** *Tale holdt for Total-Afholdenheds-Selskabet* | Publisher; the Speech is by an "anonymous member" |
| 1845 advent | **VI.** *Fire og Tyve Prædikener* | Publisher; the sermons are by Magister Sophiensen |
| 1846 Jan | **III.** *Det logiske System, Anden forøgede og forbedrede Udgave* | "Editor" (popular edition of I, with reprinted review and grateful publisher's note) |
| 1846 spring | **II.** *Lyriske Productioner for hiin Enkelte* | Publisher; lyric work by an unnamed exhausted author who addresses only the single reader |

By spring 1846 Notabene has published seven volumes, every one of them displaced through an editor- or publisher-fiction so that, technically, the prohibition has not been broken: he has written prefaces, not books. His wife has not noticed.

### The hinge — summer 1846

She notices. She has been reading the volumes herself (the Sophiensen sermons appealed to her cultivated friends) and has begun to suspect, then to know. A scene at the breakfast table; the Subscription Notice for *Bind II* of the Logical System is found beneath her tea-saucer. She does not burn anything; she simply rises, goes upstairs, packs, and leaves.

She joins the *Selskab for kvindelig Studie og Lectüre*, takes lodgings on Nørregade in the apartment of two other Society members, and files a *Skilsmissebrev* through her brother-in-law, a procurator. The grounds: persistent infidelity by means of authorship.

### Phase II — after the departure

| Year | Volume | Notabene's role |
|---|---|---|
| 1847 | **VII.** *Philosophiske Smaastykker, eller hvad et Forord kan blive til naar Bogen tages bort* | **Author.** Not "editor," not "publisher." His first signed work. Published under his own name with no framing fiction. The book is a meditation on prefaces that outlived their books, books that never appeared, promises that became substitutes for performance — and, in its closing pages, on prohibitions that walk out the door |

This makes Preface VII structurally *the only volume Notabene actually wrote*. The previous seven, despite his obvious authorship, were technically the work of others through which he merely passed as editor or publisher. VII is the work he was finally permitted to write — by the removal of the one who had permitted him only prefaces. The smuggled preface (which Kierkegaard had originally drafted for *Begrebet Angest* and then displaced into *Forord*) becomes, in our edition, the one preface Notabene authored without disguise. The Kierkegaardian joke: he writes openly only after the conditions for openness have themselves left the room.

## III. Production conceit for Wroot Press

### Format

Eight slim duodecimo or octavo volumes (sizes vary per the physical-description specs), housed in a slipcase. Optional ninth volume of biographical apparatus (see below).

### Cover briefs (already specified in each draft)

Each draft's *physical description* section is the cover brief. Pull directly from those:

- **I.** *Det logiske System* — white kid, gilt, ouroboros stamp, motto SYSTEMA EST, pale blue silk ribbon
- **II.** *Lyriske Productioner* — TBD when drafted
- **III.** *Anden Udgave* — TBD when drafted; should echo I but cheaper (board not kid, motto stamped not gilt)
- **IV.** *Urania* — white boards, gold comet whose tail forms URANIA, midnight-blue ribbon, mourning-rule frames
- **V.** *Tale for Total-Afholdenheds-Selskabet* — austere blue paper covers, inverted cup + INTET DRAABE motto, oak-leaf wreath, blue thread binding
- **VI.** *Fire og Tyve Prædikener* — dark plum cloth, blind-stamped Greek cross at centre, no gilding, black silk bookmark
- **VII.** *Philosophiske Smaastykker* — TBD when drafted, but should be the *plainest*: paper covers, no ornament, title in roman, signed "N. Notabene" in his own hand. The book that needs no costume because it is not pretending
- **VIII.** *Philosophiske Overveielser, Første Hefte* — pale grey quarto paper covers, title in heavy roman, no ornament

### Slipcase

Dark plum (matching VI), with all eight titles + author in gilt down the spine. Inside the slipcase, a printed card listing the volumes in *biographical order* (1844→1847), so the buyer can read them in the sequence Notabene wrote them rather than the order Kierkegaard listed the prefaces.

### Optional ninth volume — *Liv og Tid af Nicolaus Notabene*

A short biographical companion (~10k words) containing:
- A chronology
- A family tree (he, her, the brother-in-law procurator, the aunt at whose estate the *Forord* was written)
- The text of the wife's *Skilsmissebrev* in mock-period Danish
- A facsimile-page of the burnt *Indlednings-Paragraph* — most of it scorched away, only fragments legible
- A "Bibliography of Works Forbidden" — the lost manuscripts of 1832–43, reconstructed from such notes as survive
- Closing essay by an imagined later editor on Notabene's late silence after VII

### Imprint convention

Each volume's title page bears "FAAES HOS UNIVERSITETSBOGHANDLER C. A. REITZEL · KJØBENHAVN" as it appears in the drafts (period-faithful). The actual colophon on the verso of the title page reads "Wroot Press · Austin · Anno MMXXVI" — Wroot openly framing itself as Reitzel's late successor, not pretending otherwise.

### Print scope

Limited numbered edition (e.g., 250 sets) signed by the publisher (Wilson) on a tipped-in card. Optional cheaper unboxed paperback edition for those who want the texts without the production. Possible companion: a single-volume omnibus (~50k words) for the trade.

## IV. What still needs deciding

- Does the wife have a name? The original *Forord* never names her. Proposal: leave her unnamed throughout, including in the divorce letter and the chronology. Her power over Notabene — and over the conceit — is partly in her remaining a *function* rather than a person. She is "min Kone" and nothing more
- Does Notabene write further volumes after VII, or does the corpus close there? Proposal: VII is the last. He writes himself out. The biographical companion's closing essay can speculate (with mock-deferential restraint) on the late silence
- Print order in the slipcase: Kierkegaard's order (I–VIII) or Notabene's biographical order (IV, I, VIII, V, VI, III, II, VII)? Proposal: Kierkegaard's order on the spines, biographical order on the inserted card. The buyer chooses
- Pricing tier and whether to bundle Wroot Press patron access (newsletter, advance access to next imprint title)
- Whether to commission a translator's foreword (the texts are in English with Danish title pages; a brief note on the translation conceit may help)
